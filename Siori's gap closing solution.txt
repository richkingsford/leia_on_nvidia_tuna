Siori's Gap Closing Solution
==============================
Submitted: 2026-05-29

A proper Proportional-Derivative (PD) loop does not need to spend hours guessing or
learning from mistakes because it calculates the exact curve required to close the X, Y,
and distance errors smoothly in real-time.

Act as a senior robotics controls engineer rewriting Leia's drive code. Discard all
uncoupled loops, naive if/else distance statements, or bang-bang drive logic, as they
directly cause momentum overshoots and tread jitter. Instead, implement a coordinated
Astolfi-style approach controller for a differential-drive robot. At every 20 Hz tick,
calculate the target bearing as head_err = atan2(-x_off, y_off) and the distance error
as dist_err = distance - STOP_OFFSET. Form the coupled wheel commands where linear
velocity v = (Kp_d * dist_err + Kd_d * d_dist_err) * cos(head_err) and angular velocity
omega = Kp_h * head_err + Kd_h * d_head_err, mixing them as left = v - omega and
right = v + omega. The cos(head_err) term dynamically throttles forward speed when
misaligned so heading alignment dominates, allowing the robot to glide smoothly to a
stop without fishtailing. To preserve steering authority during high-speed deceleration,
implement coupled proportional saturation: if the maximum absolute wheel command exceeds
your physical limit, scale both wheel commands down by the exact same ratio so the
v/omega trajectory curvature survives clipping. Finally, analytically tune the PD gains
for a slightly overdamped response targeting a damping ratio of zeta = 1.5 to
mathematically guarantee zero overshoot and eliminate steady-state oscillations.

Key formulas:
  head_err  = atan2(-x_off, y_off)           [bearing to target]
  dist_err  = distance - STOP_OFFSET          [range error]
  v         = (Kp_d * dist_err + Kd_d * d_dist_err) * cos(head_err)
  omega     = Kp_h * head_err + Kd_h * d_head_err
  left      = v - omega
  right     = v + omega
  [scale both by min(1, PWM_MAX / max(|left|, |right|)) to preserve curvature]

Tuning target: zeta = 1.5 (slightly overdamped, zero overshoot guaranteed)
Tick rate: 20 Hz (50ms per tick)
